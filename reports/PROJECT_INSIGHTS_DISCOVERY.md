# Project Insights Discovery
(Pre-Phase 8 Dashboard Planning)

This document captures every important insight generated by the project from Phase 1 through Phase 7, analysed from the perspectives of Principal Reliability Engineer, Principal Maintenance Engineer, Principal Data Scientist, and Principal Business Analyst.

---

## 1. RMS Evolution as Primary Degradation Marker
- **Source Phase:** Phase 3
- **Evidence:** Mean RMS for bearing 3 increased from 0.163g to 0.194g in the final 10%; bearing 4 increased from 0.147g to 0.178g. 
- **Why it matters:** It reliably indicates advanced bearing wear and scales monotonically as failure approaches.
- **Engineering significance:** RMS acts as the anchor health indicator and the most reliable surrogate for Remaining Useful Life (RUL).
- **Maintenance significance:** Provides a highly reliable signal for late-stage wear leading up to failure, triggering immediate action.
- **Business significance:** Crucial for preventing catastrophic failure by ensuring late-stage alerts are actionable and clear.
- **Scientific confidence:** High
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Critical)

## 2. Kurtosis Non-Monotonicity (Early-Stage Sensitivity)
- **Source Phase:** Phase 3
- **Evidence:** Kurtosis peaks mid-way (e.g. 71.58 for bearing 3) and then decreases significantly in the final 10% (mean 5.84).
- **Why it matters:** Highly sensitive to impulsive events from early defects, but drops off as broadband noise overwhelms impulses later in the degradation cycle.
- **Engineering significance:** Proves that monotonicity corrections must not be applied to Kurtosis. Its decline encodes the physical transition from impulsive to broadband wear.
- **Maintenance significance:** Provides the earliest warning of defect propagation, long before RMS spikes.
- **Business significance:** Increases lead time for maintenance scheduling, reducing expedited shipping or downtime costs.
- **Scientific confidence:** High (documented physical phenomenon)
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Important)

## 3. Crest Factor as Complementary Early Indicator
- **Source Phase:** Phase 3
- **Evidence:** Extreme spikes observed early in the lifecycle (e.g. 25.95 peak for bearing 3).
- **Why it matters:** Acts as a complementary early-stage indicator to Kurtosis, normalising peaks differently to confirm impulsive events.
- **Engineering significance:** Retained in Phase 4 as an independent early-stage feature that behaves consistently with mechanical theory.
- **Maintenance significance:** Validates and confirms early-stage impulsive defects suspected by Kurtosis anomalies.
- **Business significance:** Provides additional confidence for early maintenance planning decisions.
- **Scientific confidence:** High
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Optional)

## 4. Bearing 1 Sensor Artefact
- **Source Phase:** Phase 3
- **Evidence:** Consistently negative mean vibration (-0.11582 g) across all files for Bearing 1, inflating its absolute RMS.
- **Why it matters:** It is a sensor drift artefact (DC offset) from conditioning circuitry, not a bearing defect signature.
- **Engineering significance:** Must be accounted for when comparing absolute magnitudes across different bearings, though it doesn't affect relative trend directions.
- **Maintenance significance:** Prevents false alarms on Bearing 1 due to elevated baseline readings.
- **Business significance:** Avoids unnecessary maintenance costs and downtime triggered by faulty sensor data rather than mechanical degradation.
- **Scientific confidence:** High
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Important)

## 5. Spectral Band Physical Alignments
- **Source Phase:** Phase 3
- **Evidence:** Early stages show narrow tonal content at shaft harmonics; late stages show broadband elevation. Bearing 3 showed energy concentration in the 1,000–5,000 Hz band at failure.
- **Why it matters:** Different frequency bands distinctly capture different physical phenomena (shaft harmonics vs. bearing defect frequencies vs. high-frequency resonance).
- **Engineering significance:** Validates the three distinct frequency bands (0-1k, 1k-5k, 5k-10k) used in Phase 4 as physically meaningful and geometry-agnostic features.
- **Maintenance significance:** Can help classify the specific type and evolutionary stage of a bearing defect.
- **Business significance:** Supports deeper diagnostic clarity for root-cause analysis reporting.
- **Scientific confidence:** Medium (based on qualitative checkpoint analysis)
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Important)

## 6. Strict Feature Gating Philosophy
- **Source Phase:** Phase 4
- **Evidence:** Features like `td_skewness` and `cross_xy_energy_ratio` were dynamically excluded by the Phase 4 pipeline because Phase 3 found no empirical trend.
- **Why it matters:** Prevents the inclusion of noisy, non-informative data into the machine learning pipeline.
- **Engineering significance:** Demonstrates a strict "default to EXCLUDE unless evidence found" philosophy, avoiding the estimation of arbitrary features.
- **Maintenance significance:** Reduces the likelihood of erratic predictions driven by noise.
- **Business significance:** Streamlines model complexity, reducing long-term compute and maintenance overhead.
- **Scientific confidence:** High
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** No

## 7. LOBO Cross-Validation Leakage Prevention
- **Source Phase:** Phase 5
- **Evidence:** Leave-One-Bearing-Out (LOBO) methodology was selected, creating exactly two folds with completely disjoint train/test units.
- **Why it matters:** It is the only methodology that properly answers the real-world generalization question: can a model trained on past failed bearings predict the RUL of a completely unseen failed bearing?
- **Engineering significance:** Leakage-free by construction, rendering rolling-window boundaries irrelevant to train/test contamination.
- **Maintenance significance:** Ensures the AI methodology precisely matches the operational deployment scenario.
- **Business significance:** Prevents false confidence and catastrophic business planning based on overly optimistic, leaky model evaluations.
- **Scientific confidence:** High
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Critical)

## 8. Small Sample Size (n=4 Caveat)
- **Source Phase:** Phase 5 (propagated through Phase 6 and 7); updated from n=2 to n=4 after the Set 2/Set 3 multi-dataset expansion
- **Evidence:** The dataset now contains four run-to-failure trajectories across three datasets: `set1::bearing_3` (inner race), `set1::bearing_4` (rolling element), `set2::bearing_1` (outer race), `set3::bearing_3` (outer race).
- **Why it matters:** Statistical performance estimates are inherently limited and represent methodology validation, not fleet-level accuracy guarantees.
- **Engineering significance:** Results cannot scientifically carry classical confidence intervals, even at n=4.
- **Maintenance significance:** Outputs must be interpreted qualitatively as directional trends rather than precise time-to-failure guarantees.
- **Business significance:** Sets highly realistic expectations for the system's current production capability; the dataset expansion (Set 1 → Set 1+2+3) is documented evidence that this limitation is being actively addressed, not ignored.
- **Scientific confidence:** High (statistical certainty)
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Critical)

## 9. Baseline Model Selection (Mean Retained, Not "No Signal")
- **Source Phase:** Phase 6 (updated per `ENGINEERING_DECISIONS.md` ED-006, multi-dataset n=4 re-run)
- **Evidence:** XGBoost actually **beats** the mean baseline on this data — ~24% lower mean MAE (12,589 vs 16,539 minutes) and up to ~90% lower near-failure MAE in some folds. It is not promoted to production: LOBO CV performance varies across the three datasets' different life scales, so no candidate beats every rival on every fold — not even the median baseline, which now loses to mean specifically on the largest fold (`set3::bearing_3`, 6,323 rows). The demotion ladder therefore falls back one rung further than at n=2 (Mean, not Median).
- **Why it matters:** This is a fold-structure and cross-dataset-heterogeneity artifact, not evidence that the engineered features lack degradation signal — the flexible models' advantage documented at n=2 (ED-004) not only survived the multi-dataset expansion, it widened. The distinction matters because it changes the correct next step from "the features don't work" to "the every-fold-beat rule is a genuinely hard bar across heterogeneous datasets."
- **Engineering significance:** The demotion rule worked exactly as designed (conservative-by-default under insufficient evidence), but the *reason* must be reported precisely — XGBoost is documented as the leading promotion candidate, not discarded as a failure. See ED-006 for the fold-by-fold evidence table.
- **Maintenance significance:** The system provides a constant, conservative baseline today, while the pipeline has already identified which model to promote pending a fold-structure review or additional trajectories.
- **Business significance:** Reframes the investment case: this is not "the ML approach failed," it's "the ML approach works, and the promotion bar (every-fold-beat across heterogeneous datasets) is intentionally strict."
- **Scientific confidence:** High (follows rigorous architectural promotion rules; evidence table in `ENGINEERING_DECISIONS.md` ED-006)
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Important)

## 10. Baseline Model Biases (Early vs Late Warning)
- **Source Phase:** Phase 6
- **Evidence:** The `mean` baseline over-predicts (mean signed error +4262.31 min, MAE 16,538.85 min, creating late warnings), while the `median` baseline under-predicts less severely (mean signed error +1445.97 min, MAE 16,149.83 min).
- **Why it matters:** The inherent bias of the selected model directly dictates the risk profile of the maintenance alerts.
- **Engineering significance:** Because `mean` was selected, the system is mathematically skewed towards late, less conservative warnings — the opposite direction from the n=2/median-selected result, and worth flagging explicitly to maintenance stakeholders.
- **Maintenance significance:** Results in a less conservative operational profile with less lead time than the previous (n=2) median-selected baseline provided.
- **Business significance:** The direction of systematic bias changed with the dataset expansion — a concrete example of why model selection outcomes must be re-validated whenever the training population changes, not assumed to persist.
- **Scientific confidence:** High
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Optional)

## 11. Feature Importance Physical Alignment
- **Source Phase:** Phase 6
- **Evidence:** `td_rms` is consistently ranked as the top feature across permutation importance and SHAP, followed by `td_kurtosis` and frequency band energies.
- **Why it matters:** Independent ML interpretability techniques explicitly confirm the human engineering findings from Phase 3.
- **Engineering significance:** Validates the feature engineering pipeline and the physical grounding of the data.
- **Maintenance significance:** Maintenance engineers can trust that the AI is paying attention to the correct, physically sound degradation markers.
- **Business significance:** Ensures the AI is highly traceable and interpretable, fostering stakeholder trust.
- **Scientific confidence:** Medium (constrained by n=2, but physically robust)
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Optional)

## 12. Categorical Confidence Limits
- **Source Phase:** Phase 7
- **Evidence:** System confidence is restricted to Categorical values only (e.g., LOW). Numerical probability estimates were disabled.
- **Why it matters:** Numerical probability estimates are statistically unsupportable for an n=2 corpus.
- **Engineering significance:** Directly enforces statistical governance architecture rules regarding small datasets.
- **Maintenance significance:** Prevents maintenance personnel from over-relying on falsely precise exact numbers.
- **Business significance:** Protects organizational liability by clearly and transparently stating the AI system's limitations.
- **Scientific confidence:** High
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Critical)

## 13. Uniform Decision Profile
- **Source Phase:** Phase 7
- **Evidence:** 4,312 out of 4,312 maintenance recommendations are mapped to SCHEDULE_MAINTENANCE with a MEDIUM risk category.
- **Why it matters:** Due to the fallback to the constant median baseline model, the system currently acts as a static policy rather than a dynamic predictor.
- **Engineering significance:** Proves the business rule engine processes baseline predictions safely and consistently.
- **Maintenance significance:** The system is not currently providing differentiated, dynamic warnings over time.
- **Business significance:** Highlights the immediate business need for more run-to-failure training data to unlock the dynamic predictive value of the ML pipeline.
- **Scientific confidence:** High
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Important)

## 14. Placeholder Risk Thresholds
- **Source Phase:** Phase 7
- **Evidence:** Thresholds determining maintenance actions (e.g., CRITICAL at 48 hours, MEDIUM at 336 hours) are currently engineering placeholders.
- **Why it matters:** They govern the severity of maintenance actions but have not yet been field-calibrated by domain experts.
- **Engineering significance:** These values must be actively updated and validated before full operational deployment.
- **Maintenance significance:** Requires review and sign-off by senior reliability engineers based on operational realities.
- **Business significance:** A crucial calibration step required to accurately optimize maintenance ROI and minimize false alarms.
- **Scientific confidence:** Low (acknowledged placeholder status)
- **Whether it should appear on the final dashboard:** Yes (Important)

## Categorized Insights

### 1. Executive Insights
- **Small Sample Size (n=4 Caveat):** The dataset severely limits statistical guarantees; output is methodology validation, not a fleet-level accuracy guarantee.
- **Categorical Confidence Limits:** To protect liability and prevent false precision, confidence metrics are restricted to categorical labels (LOW) rather than precise probabilities.

### 2. Engineering Insights
- **Bearing 1 Sensor Artefact:** Bearing 1 exhibits a DC offset artefact that inflates absolute RMS but does not reflect actual bearing degradation.
- **Spectral Band Physical Alignments:** The defined frequency bands capture distinct physical phenomena (shaft harmonics, defects, resonance) accurately.
- **Strict Feature Gating Philosophy:** Features without Phase 3 empirical evidence were strictly excluded, protecting pipeline integrity.

### 3. Machine Learning Insights
- **LOBO Cross-Validation Leakage Prevention:** Leave-One-Bearing-Out is the only valid, leakage-free methodology for this corpus.
- **Baseline Model Selection (Mean Retained, Not "No Signal"):** XGBoost beats the mean baseline (~24% mean MAE, up to ~90% near-failure MAE) but can't be promoted under the every-fold-beat rule across 3 heterogeneous datasets; a conservative mean baseline governs production pending a fold-structure review or more failure trajectories.
- **Feature Importance Physical Alignment:** SHAP and permutation importance perfectly align with physical engineering reality, identifying RMS and Kurtosis as top drivers.

### 4. Reliability Insights
- **RMS Evolution as Primary Degradation Marker:** RMS is the anchor indicator for late-stage, macroscopic bearing wear.
- **Kurtosis Non-Monotonicity (Early-Stage Sensitivity):** Kurtosis provides the earliest warning for localized impacts but physically must drop as damage becomes broadband.
- **Crest Factor as Complementary Early Indicator:** Crest factor spikes reliably confirm the early-stage impulsive events detected by Kurtosis.

### 5. Maintenance Insights
- **Baseline Model Biases (Early vs Late Warning):** The selected mean model mathematically produces later, less conservative maintenance warnings than the previous (n=2) median-selected baseline.

### 6. Business Insights
- **Uniform Decision Profile:** Because the model fell back to a constant mean, current maintenance recommendations are uniformly static. More run-to-failure data or a fold-structure review is required to unlock dynamic ML value.

### 7. Governance Insights
- **Small Sample Size (n=4 Caveat):** Enforced architectural governance dictates that the n=2 limitation must visibly travel with all predictions.
- **Categorical Confidence Limits:** Governance protocols disabled numerical probabilities to prevent scientifically invalid assertions.

### 8. Operational Insights
- **Placeholder Risk Thresholds:** The hour/day risk thresholds triggering maintenance actions require operational domain-expert calibration prior to deployment.
